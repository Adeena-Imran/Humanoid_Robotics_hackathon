# Sensor Types in Humanoid Robots

## Introduction

Humanoid robots rely heavily on a diverse array of sensors to perceive their own state (proprioception) and their surrounding environment (exteroception). These sensors are the robot's "eyes," "ears," and "skin," providing the critical data needed for tasks like balancing, walking, object manipulation, and navigation.

Understanding the different types of sensors, their working principles, and their respective advantages and limitations is fundamental to designing and controlling humanoid robots effectively.

## Proprioceptive Sensors

These sensors provide information about the robot's internal state.

### 1. Joint Encoders
*   **Purpose**: Measure the angular position or velocity of a robot's joints.
*   **Examples**: Optical encoders, potentiometers.
*   **Advantages**: High precision, direct measurement of joint state.
*   **Limitations**: Does not directly measure external forces or absolute position.

### 2. Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs)
*   **Purpose**: Measure orientation, angular velocity, and linear acceleration.
*   **Components**: Accelerometers, gyroscopes, magnetometers.
*   **Advantages**: Compact, provides crucial information for balance and attitude control.
*   **Limitations**: Suffers from drift over time, sensitive to vibrations.

### 3. Force/Torque (F/T) Sensors
*   **Purpose**: Measure forces and torques applied at specific points (e.g., wrists, ankles).
*   **Examples**: Strain gauge-based sensors.
*   **Advantages**: Crucial for compliant interaction, grasping, and estimating ground reaction forces.
*   **Limitations**: Can be delicate, sensitive to temperature changes.

## Exteroceptive Sensors

These sensors provide information about the robot's external environment.

### 1. Cameras (Vision Sensors)
*   **Purpose**: Capture visual information for object recognition, navigation, and human-robot interaction.
*   **Types**: Monocular, stereo, depth cameras (RGB-D).
*   **Advantages**: Rich information, human-like perception.
*   **Limitations**: Sensitive to lighting conditions, computationally intensive processing.

### 2. LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging)
*   **Purpose**: Measure distances to objects by emitting pulsed laser light.
*   **Outputs**: 2D or 3D point clouds.
*   **Advantages**: Accurate depth information, works in various lighting.
*   **Limitations**: Can be affected by adverse weather, expensive.

### 3. Proximity Sensors
*   **Purpose**: Detect the presence of nearby objects without physical contact.
*   **Types**: Infrared, ultrasonic.
*   **Advantages**: Simple, low-cost, effective for collision avoidance at short ranges.
*   **Limitations**: Limited range, can be fooled by certain surfaces.

### 4. Tactile Sensors
*   **Purpose**: Detect physical contact and pressure.
*   **Examples**: Pressure arrays on fingertips, force-sensing resistors.
*   **Advantages**: Enables delicate manipulation and safe physical interaction.
*   **Limitations**: Durability, complex wiring.
