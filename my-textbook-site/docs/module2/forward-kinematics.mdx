# Forward Kinematics of Humanoid Robots

## Introduction

Forward kinematics (FK) is a fundamental concept in robotics that deals with determining the position and orientation of the robot's end-effector (e.g., hand, foot) given the joint angles (or positions) of its links. Understanding FK is crucial for tasks such as robot control, trajectory planning, and workspace analysis.

In humanoid robots, FK allows us to know exactly where the robot's body parts are in space as its joints move. This is essential for preventing collisions, performing precise tasks, and understanding the robot's capabilities.

## The Forward Kinematics Problem

Consider a robotic arm made up of several rigid links connected by several joints. Each joint allows a certain degree of freedom (e.g., rotation or translation). The FK problem can be formulated as:

> Given the geometric parameters of each link and joint, and the current angles/positions of all joints, compute the position and orientation of the end-effector with respect to a fixed base frame.

This typically involves a series of transformations (rotations and translations) along the robot's kinematic chain.

## Key Concepts

*   **Links**: The rigid bodies that make up the robot structure.
*   **Joints**: Connections between links that allow relative motion.
*   **Degrees of Freedom (DOF)**: The number of independent parameters that define the configuration of a system.
*   **Kinematic Chain**: The sequence of links and joints from the base to the end-effector.
*   **End-Effector**: The part of the robot that performs tasks.
*   **Homogeneous Transformation Matrices**: Mathematical tools used to represent and combine translations and rotations.
