# Inverse Kinematics of Humanoid Robots

## Introduction

Inverse kinematics (IK) is another critical concept in robotics, complementary to forward kinematics. While forward kinematics determines the end-effector pose from joint angles, inverse kinematics solves the opposite problem:

> Given a desired position and orientation for the robot's end-effector, what are the corresponding joint angles (or positions) that would achieve this pose?

IK is essential for making robots perform tasks where the desired end-effector position is known, such as grasping an object, walking, or interacting with a user at a specific point in space.

## The Inverse Kinematics Problem

The IK problem is often more complex than FK because:
1.  **Multiple Solutions**: For a given end-effector pose, there might be several sets of joint angles that can achieve it.
2.  **No Solution**: The desired pose might be physically unreachable by the robot (outside its workspace).
3.  **Computational Complexity**: Solving IK can involve complex non-linear equations, often requiring iterative numerical methods.

## Key Concepts

*   **Workspace**: The set of all points in space that the robot's end-effector can reach.
*   **Redundancy**: Having more degrees of freedom than strictly necessary to achieve a task, leading to multiple IK solutions.
*   **Kinematic Singularities**: Configurations where the robot loses one or more degrees of freedom, making certain movements impossible or requiring very large joint velocities.
*   **Analytical vs. Numerical IK**: Different approaches to solving IK equations.
